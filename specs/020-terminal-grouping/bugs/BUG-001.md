## Description

The Overseer terminal-group hierarchy is present, but its current presentation is difficult to scan and operate in the left sidebar. In the supplied screenshot (`/Users/olegbalunenko/Desktop/Screenshot 2026-08-25 at 15.19.34.png`), the fixed-width terminal panel places the group name and rename, move-up, move-down, and dissolve controls on one row. The group name is reduced to `МАРШ...`, and abbreviated action labels such as `ПЕРЕИМ.` and `РАСФОРМ.` do not provide enough context at a glance.

Each terminal member also places its name and several management actions into the same narrow card. Rename, move, ordering, and delete controls compete for horizontal space; labels are clipped and controls at the right edge are difficult to identify or can become visually unreachable. The result technically satisfies the structural group hierarchy, but the Overseer cannot confidently distinguish the selected group or terminal from the action about to be performed. The proximity of routine ordering actions and destructive actions increases the risk of choosing the wrong operation.

The current implementation makes the issue reproducible by combining a fixed `240px` `.term-panel` with always-visible group and terminal action rows. Group names intentionally use ellipsis, while the terminal action row has no wrapping, overflow menu, or comparable space-management strategy. Existing browser coverage validates structure and behavior but does not assert that labels and controls remain readable and reachable at supported viewport sizes or with realistic Russian names.

## Root Cause Analysis

The feature specification concentrated on membership invariants, persistence, navigation safety, atomic mutations, and confirmation behavior. It required a high-level hierarchy but never translated “usable group management” into measurable presentation constraints. The plan and tasks then used broad terms such as “accessible” and “responsive” without an acceptance criterion that could fail on visual density.

The implementation followed the easiest direct-control model: a fixed-width legacy sidebar with every group and terminal action rendered inline. Functional browser tests confirmed the expected elements, ordering, and mutations, so the work appeared complete even though no test evaluated readable names, control overflow, action clarity, or realistic localized content. This is primarily a specification gap with a resulting frontend layout deficiency.
